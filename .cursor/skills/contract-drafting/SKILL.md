# Contract Drafting

## Scope

Use this skill for software development service contracts, statements of work, quotation sheets, maintenance agreements, change orders, and contract review notes.

This skill provides practical drafting support, not legal advice. Formal contracts should be reviewed by qualified legal and tax professionals before signing.

## Workflow

1. Confirm deal context:
   - Parties, project type, buyer background, relationship sensitivity, deal amount, tax/invoice treatment, and signing deadline.
   - Identify whether the user is the developer/vendor or client/buyer.

2. Define commercial structure:
   - Separate development fee, third-party pass-through costs, warranty, maintenance, operations support, and future iteration fees.
   - Separate included scope, excluded scope, optional scope, and post-launch services.

3. Draft core terms:
   - Project positioning and deliverables.
   - Fee schedule and payment milestones.
   - Third-party fees and account ownership.
   - Acceptance criteria and acceptance window.
   - Warranty scope and exclusions.
   - Maintenance scope, response boundaries, and service hours.
   - Change-order process.
   - IP/source-code delivery.
   - Data/privacy responsibilities.
   - Suspension, termination, and dispute resolution.

4. Allocate risk explicitly:
   - Client-owned assets: domain, cloud account, mini program account, payment account, filing/备案, business licenses, and operating data.
   - Developer responsibilities: agreed implementation, technical integration, deployment assistance, bug fixes within warranty scope.
   - Client responsibilities: materials, credentials, qualification documents, business rules, legal texts, renewals, third-party platform payments.

5. Check for dangerous ambiguity:
   - "全年质保" must distinguish bug warranty from new features, operations, design work, and business-rule changes.
   - "运维" must distinguish technical maintenance from content operations, on-call support, campaign configuration, and design support.
   - "总包" must state whether it includes tax, third-party fees, annual services, or future iterations.
   - "源码交付" must state timing, condition, and whether pre-existing developer tools/components are excluded.

## Output Format

When drafting a contract, use:

1. Contract title and disclaimer.
2. Parties and project positioning.
3. Scope included and excluded.
4. Fee schedule and payment milestones.
5. Third-party fees and account ownership.
6. Warranty and maintenance.
7. Deliverables and acceptance.
8. Change-order process.
9. IP/source-code, confidentiality, data/privacy.
10. Liability boundaries, suspension/termination, dispute resolution.
11. Attachments: feature boundary, fee table, client materials checklist, negotiation notes.

When reviewing a contract, lead with:

1. Top risks.
2. Must-change clauses.
3. Negotiable clauses.
4. Suggested replacement language.
5. Open questions for the user.

## Drafting Rules

- Prefer clear Chinese over legalese.
- State every amount, tax assumption, payment trigger, and excluded item explicitly.
- Do not hide uncertain legal, tax, or regulatory points; mark them for professional review.
- Avoid promising unlimited support, 24-hour operations, or policy compliance unless priced and scoped.
- Keep goodwill language separate from binding obligations.
- Use appendices for long feature lists and fee tables so the main contract stays readable.
